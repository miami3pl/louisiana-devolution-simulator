Class 5 · who is even on the tree — filiation and adoption

This calculator assumes everyone shown is filiated. That is the question Class 5 answers, and it decides whether a person belongs on the tree at all — no filiation, no heir.

Adoption cuts one way — art. 199 On adoption the adopting parent becomes the parent for all purposes and filiation to the former legal parent is terminated — but "the adopted child and his descendants retain the right to inherit from his former legal parent and the relatives of that parent." It does not run in reverse: the biological parent is cut off.

So an adopted child sits in TWO trees for his own inheritance rights, and in ONE for everybody else's. He inherits up both; only the adoptive side inherits down.
Filiation — arts. 178, 179 Filiation is the legal relationship between child and parent, established by proof of maternity, paternity, or adoption. Maternity (art. 184) is a preponderance that the child was born of a particular woman — no blood testing needed. Paternity is where the work is.
Three presumptions of paternity 1 · Husband of the mother (art. 185) — the "strongest presumption in Louisiana law"; covers a child born during the marriage or within 300 days of its end. Two husbands? Art. 186 picks H1, unless he disavows.
2 · Marriage + acknowledgment (art. 195) — marries the mother of a child not filiated to another man and acknowledges by authentic act.
3 · Formal acknowledgment (art. 196) — by authentic act; runs in the child's favour only: the acknowledging father cannot inherit from the child or sue for wrongful death.
Three ways to defeat one Disavowal (arts. 187–190) — clear and convincing, corroborated; heritable; prescriptive.
Contestation by the mother (arts. 191–193) — needs H2's authentic act and clear and convincing proof; 2 years and 180 days, both.
Three-party acknowledgment (art. 190.1) — mother, presumed father and biological father sign; needs 99.9% probability; 10 years.
Two court actions — and the trap The child's action (art. 197): preponderance if the alleged father lives, clear and convincing if he is dead; any time — except for succession purposes, where it is a one-year peremption from the death.
The father's avowal (art. 198): strictly personal, not heritable; one year from birth if the child is presumed another man's; never later than one year from the child's death.

The pattern worth saying out loud: the only prescriptive periods here are the disavowal ones — the action that destroys a filiation. Everything that creates or redirects one is peremptive and dies on the day. The Code forgives a late attack on paternity more readily than a late claim to it.

Reference · before or after — the whole of Chapter 3

Same family, same shares, opposite routes. Representation is fenced — descendants and siblings only. Transmission is open to anyone, because nothing is being pretended: the rights really did vest.

5 · Why — the ladder, rung by rung (skipped rungs marked empty)

    Models: intestate devolution — arts. 888–896, 902 (separate) and 889–890 (community); the half-blood line split of art. 893; the parental usufruct of art. 891; representation in the direct line (art. 882) and the collateral line (art. 884), partitioned by roots (art. 885); and transmission (art. 937) where an heir survives the decedent and then dies.

    Not modelled: forced heirship and the legitime; collation; putative spouses; judicial separation as a variable; the art. 1514 security question; and unworthiness (art. 941), though his slide notes the unworthy and those who renounce are treated as if they predeceased.
